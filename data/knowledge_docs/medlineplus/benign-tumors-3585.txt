Title: Benign Tumors
Source: MedlinePlus
URL: https://medlineplus.gov/benigntumors.html
Language: English
Also called: Benign neoplasms, Noncancerous tumors
Health topic groups: Cancers

Summary:
Tumors are abnormal growths in your body. They can be either benign or malignant. Benign tumors
aren't cancer. Malignant ones are. Benign tumors grow only in one place. They cannot spread or
invade other parts of your body. Even so, they can be dangerous if they press on vital organs, such
as your brain. Tumors are made up of extra cells. Normally, cells grow and divide to form new cells
as your body needs them. When cells grow old, they die, and new cells take their place. Sometimes,
this process goes wrong. New cells form when your body does not need them, and old cells do not die
when they should. These extra cells can divide without stopping and may form a tumor. Treatment
often involves surgery. Benign tumors usually don't grow back. NIH: National Cancer Institute
